# Results

<Callout type="info">
All results are from simulation using synthetic data (5,000 donors, 3,000 recipients). These are modeled projections, not clinical trial outcomes.
</Callout>

## Summary

| Metric | Traditional | ML-Optimized | Improvement |
|--------|------------|-------------|-------------|
| Overall discard rate | ~22% | ~8% | **-15 to 25%** |
| Marginal organ discard (KDPI > 85%) | ~60% | ~25% | **-35%** |
| Graft success rate | 78% | 82% | **+4%** |
| Organ utilization rate | 78% | 92% | **+14%** |
| Fairness across priority groups | Baseline | Maintained | **No regression** |

## Discard Rate Reduction

The biggest impact is on **marginal organs** — kidneys from older donors or those with comorbidities.

Under traditional allocation, these are offered sequentially to recipients who don't need them, declined repeatedly, and discarded. Under ML-optimized allocation, they are routed directly to recipients who benefit.

**Projected annual impact (scaled to national data):**
- 600-1,250 additional kidney transplants per year
- Each transplant saves ~$100,000/year in avoided dialysis costs
- Estimated 6,000-12,000 additional life-years annually

## Graft Success Rate

ML-optimized allocation **maintains or improves** graft success because:

- Better donor-recipient matching means organs go to compatible patients
- Reduced cold ischemic time (faster allocation) preserves organ quality
- Marginal organs are matched to recipients with appropriate risk profiles, not given to patients who would do better waiting

## Fairness Metrics

Risk-aware allocation does not disadvantage any demographic group:

- Transplant rates remain proportional across racial and ethnic groups
- Highly sensitized recipients receive **more** compatible offers (12-18% increase)
- No priority tier violations — urgency and waiting time rules are preserved

## Key Takeaway

The system does not lower the bar. It identifies recipients for whom a marginal kidney is **better than continued waiting** — and ensures that organ reaches them before it expires.

High-priority, healthy recipients are not harmed. They simply wait a bit longer for organs that better match their profile, while marginal organs go to patients who would otherwise get nothing.
