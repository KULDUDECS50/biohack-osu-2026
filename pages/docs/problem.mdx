# The Problem

## Kidneys are being wasted

Over 100,000 patients are on the U.S. transplant waiting list. More than 6,000 die each year waiting. Yet **20% of recovered kidneys are discarded** — roughly 4,000-5,000 organs annually.

These organs are not unusable. They are *marginal* — slightly older donors, minor comorbidities, longer ischemic times — and the current system has no good way to place them.

## Why it happens

### Binary thresholds

Organs are scored using the **Kidney Donor Profile Index (KDPI)**, a 0-100% composite metric. Higher KDPI means lower quality.

<Callout type="warning">
Organs with KDPI above 85% face discard rates of roughly 60%, even though many could function well in appropriate recipients.
</Callout>

The problem: KDPI is treated as a pass/fail gate. An organ at KDPI 84% gets accepted. One at 86% gets thrown away. The biological difference is minimal.

### Sequential "first-in-line" offers

When an organ becomes available, it is offered to recipients one at a time, starting at the top of the match list.

- Recipient #1 declines (organ too old)
- Recipient #2 declines (center protocol)
- Recipient #3 declines (surgeon unavailable)
- ... hours pass, ischemic time increases, quality degrades
- Eventually the organ is discarded

Each decline wastes time and worsens the organ.

### Center-level risk aversion

Transplant centers are measured on one-year graft survival. Accepting a marginal organ that fails hurts their metrics. So they decline — even when the organ could help a patient for whom waiting is more dangerous.

## The tradeoff nobody sees

For many patients, **waiting is not safe**:

- Dialysis patients face 15-20% annual mortality
- Health deteriorates over time, reducing transplant candidacy
- A marginal kidney today may be better than a perfect kidney that never comes

Current systems do not model this tradeoff. They optimize for organ quality in isolation, ignoring recipient-specific risk.

## What's needed

A system that:

- Treats organ quality as a **spectrum**, not a binary
- Matches organ risk to **recipient risk tolerance**
- Reduces time wasted on sequential declines
- Keeps all existing fairness and priority protections
