# Recipient Matching

## Why matching matters

Not every patient needs a perfect kidney. Not every patient can afford to wait for one.

The matching engine dynamically reorders the candidate list for each organ, prioritizing recipients who derive the **greatest survival benefit** from *this specific kidney* — not just whoever has waited longest.

## Who benefits from marginal organs?

Different recipients have different risk tolerances:

| Recipient Profile | Risk Tolerance | Rationale |
|-------------------|---------------|-----------|
| Age 60+, long dialysis history | **High** | Waiting is more dangerous than a marginal kidney |
| Highly sensitized (high PRA) | **High** | May never get a better offer due to immunological barriers |
| Young, healthy, first transplant | **Low** | Likely to receive multiple good offers; worth waiting |
| Rare blood type, remote location | **High** | Few compatible organs reach them |

## How ranking works

For each donor kidney, the system:

1. **Filters** for ABO blood type compatibility and basic eligibility
2. **Preserves** existing priority tiers (medical urgency, pediatric status, waiting time)
3. **Calculates** a utility score for each candidate based on:
   - Predicted graft success for this donor-recipient pair
   - Recipient's waitlist mortality risk
   - Likelihood of a better offer coming soon
4. **Reorders** recipients within priority tiers by utility score

<Callout>
No recipient ever loses their priority tier. Reordering happens *within* tiers only. A Status 1A patient always ranks above a standard-status patient.
</Callout>

## Example

**Donor kidney: viability score 66 (marginal)**

| Recipient | Age | Dialysis Years | Traditional Rank | ML Rank | Why |
|-----------|-----|---------------|-----------------|---------|-----|
| A | 35 | 2 | #1 | #3 | Young, will get better offers soon |
| B | 62 | 5 | #3 | #1 | High waitlist mortality, benefits most |
| C | 58 | 4 | #2 | #2 | Highly sensitized, few future offers |

Recipient B — who would die waiting under the old system — gets the kidney. Recipient A waits a few more weeks and gets a better one.

Both patients are better off.

## Safeguards

- **Maximum position change:** No recipient drops more than 10 positions due to reordering
- **Equity monitoring:** Allocation outcomes tracked by demographics to prevent systematic bias
- **Override capability:** Clinicians can always override the algorithmic ranking with documented justification
- **Transparency:** Full ranking rationale is logged and auditable
