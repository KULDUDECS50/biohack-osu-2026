# Viability Scoring

## The core idea

Instead of classifying kidneys as "good" or "bad," the model outputs a **continuous viability score from 0 to 100** — capturing the full spectrum of organ quality.

This score predicts graft success probability and enables nuanced matching rather than hard cutoffs.

## Input Features

The model uses these donor characteristics:

| Feature | Type | Why It Matters |
|---------|------|----------------|
| Age | Numeric | Primary predictor of graft longevity |
| BMI | Numeric | Organ size and functional reserve |
| Hypertension | Binary | Vascular disease indicator |
| Diabetes | Binary | Microvascular damage risk |
| Smoking history | Binary | Cardiovascular and organ quality |
| Cause of death | Categorical | Ischemic injury burden |
| Serum creatinine | Numeric | Direct kidney function measure |
| KDPI | Numeric | Composite donor quality index |
| Cold ischemic time | Numeric | Preservation duration impact |

## Score Interpretation

| Score Range | Quality | One-Year Graft Survival |
|-------------|---------|------------------------|
| 90-100 | Excellent | ~95% |
| 70-89 | Good | ~85% |
| 50-69 | Marginal | ~70% |
| Below 50 | High risk | Below 70% |

<Callout type="warning">
A score of 65 is not "unacceptable." It means the kidney has elevated risk that may be appropriate for certain recipients — especially those for whom waiting is more dangerous.
</Callout>

## Example

**62-year-old donor, controlled hypertension, no diabetes, creatinine 1.3 mg/dL, KDPI 75%**

- **Viability score: 68** (marginal range)
- Predicted graft success: 79% for medium-risk recipients
- Under traditional rules: likely declined by multiple centers, high discard risk
- Under ML allocation: routed to an older recipient with 5+ years on dialysis who benefits significantly

## Model Details

**Algorithm:** XGBoost (gradient-boosted decision trees)

**Why XGBoost:**
- Strong performance on tabular clinical data
- Handles missing values natively
- Feature importance is interpretable
- Proven in clinical risk prediction

**Training:**
- Five-fold cross-validation on synthetic data
- Trained on donor features with graft outcome as target
- C-statistic: 0.74 (good discrimination for clinical models)

**Key design choice:** We prioritize interpretability over marginal accuracy gains. Clinicians need to understand *why* a score is what it is, not just trust a number.

## No Hard Cutoffs

Traditional KDPI creates a cliff effect:

- KDPI 84% → accepted
- KDPI 86% → discarded

The viability model produces **smooth gradients**. Small changes in donor characteristics produce small changes in score — no arbitrary cliffs.

Centers can set their own acceptable score ranges based on their patient population and expertise, rather than following national binary cutoffs.
